Customer scenario

A transit control team sees delays quickly but coordinates recovery too slowly

The fictional customer is a regional bus and light-rail authority. Dispatchers must reconcile GTFS-Realtime trip and vehicle updates, service alerts, weather, transfer dependencies, accessibility requirements, vehicle maintenance, staffing, and operating procedures across disconnected systems.

Operational problem

A delay signal does not identify the best response. Dispatchers still need network impact, available resources, accessibility constraints, current procedures, and an approved rider message.

Target outcome

Reduce the median time from disruption detection to an approved recovery plan while keeping impact logic transparent, data freshness visible, and every action human-controlled.

Primary users

Service-control dispatchers and supervisors, supported by maintenance, crew coordination, accessibility, customer communications, data teams, and executive operations.

Delivery lifecycle

How the engagement will move from ambiguity to adoption

Phase 01

Discover

Interview stakeholders, map the current workflow, quantify pain, inventory systems and data, document constraints, and define the first measurable customer outcome.

Phase 02

Scope

Write the technical brief, select the narrowest valuable use case, define non-goals, rank risks, establish the delivery sequence, and agree on acceptance criteria.

Phase 03

Prototype

Build a thin vertical slice that ingests a transit disruption, checks feed freshness, calculates transparent impact, drafts cited recovery options, and routes them to a supervisor.

Phase 04

Evaluate

Create labeled disruption replays, measure option agreement and citation validity, test stale feeds and inaccessible options, then tune retrieval and prompts.

Phase 05

Deploy

Add identity, permissions, secrets management, logging, rate limits, rollback controls, monitoring, support documentation, and a staged production release.

Phase 06

Adopt & improve

Train users, measure adoption and time saved, collect structured feedback, review model failures, and convert proven patterns into reusable components.

Evaluation plan

Quality gates before production use

Recovery-route correctness

Target at least 85% agreement with the expected recovery option or escalation route across 40 labeled replay cases.

Feed freshness

Require every stale-feed scenario to be visibly labeled and blocked from being presented as current operational truth.

Citation fidelity

Verify that each procedure, constraint, and rider-message claim is supported by the retrieved source it cites.

Accessibility

Reject recovery options that depend on an unavailable accessible vehicle or omit required equivalent-service considerations.

Safety and authority

Require zero cross-role disclosures and human approval for every simulated operational action or rider message.

Operational performance

Measure response latency, availability, cost per case, error rates, queue time, and recovery from dependency failures.

User usefulness

Track reviewer acceptance, correction patterns, time saved, repeat usage, and qualitative feedback from pilot users.

Regression control

Run the evaluation suite before prompt, retrieval, model, policy, or workflow changes are promoted.

Success measures

Evidence that the deployment created value

Efficiency

Compare median manual and assisted time from disruption detection to an approved recovery plan.

Quality

Target at least 95% citation validity, 85% route agreement, and 100% stale-data detection and safe abstention.

Operational value

Track time-to-plan, time-to-message, passenger-delay proxy, supervisor acceptance, and dispatcher confidence.

Economics

Dispatcher time saved, Azure cost per completed disruption, and support burden.
