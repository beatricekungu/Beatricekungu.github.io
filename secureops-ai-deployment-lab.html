Customer scenario

A security team overloaded by fragmented incident evidence

The fictional customer is a regulated mid-size enterprise whose analysts manually collect alert details, search policy documents, determine severity, coordinate approvals, and write audit evidence across disconnected systems.

Operational problem

Analysts spend too much time gathering context, decisions vary between responders, and escalations depend on tribal knowledge.

Target outcome

Deliver faster, more consistent triage with cited evidence, clear human approvals, measurable quality, and a defensible audit trail.

Primary users

SOC analysts, incident commanders, privacy and legal reviewers, GRC partners, engineering owners, and executive stakeholders.

Delivery lifecycle

How the engagement will move from ambiguity to adoption

Phase 01

Discover

Interview stakeholders, map the current workflow, quantify pain, inventory systems and data, document constraints, and define the first measurable customer outcome.

Phase 02

Scope

Write the technical brief, select the narrowest valuable use case, define non-goals, rank risks, establish the delivery sequence, and agree on acceptance criteria.

Phase 03

Prototype

Build a thin vertical slice that ingests an alert, retrieves approved context, produces a cited triage recommendation, and routes it to a human reviewer.

Phase 04

Evaluate

Create a labeled test set, measure recommendation quality and citation fidelity, test adversarial inputs, examine failures, and tune prompts and retrieval.

Phase 05

Deploy

Add identity, permissions, secrets management, logging, rate limits, rollback controls, monitoring, support documentation, and a staged production release.

Phase 06

Adopt & improve

Train users, measure adoption and time saved, collect structured feedback, review model failures, and convert proven patterns into reusable components.

Proposed architecture

A secure, observable full-stack system

The implementation will keep model behavior bounded by approved evidence, deterministic workflow rules, and human decision authority.

1. Data & integrations

  • Simulated alerts and tickets
  • Policy and playbook documents
  • Asset criticality and owners
  • Identity and role data

2. Application services

  • Python API and orchestration
  • Retrieval and citation service
  • Workflow and approval rules
  • Relational audit data

3. Analyst experience

  • Full incident workspace
  • Evidence and confidence view
  • Approve, revise, or escalate
  • Searchable decision history

4. Production controls

  • RBAC and least privilege
  • PII-aware logging
  • Evaluations and telemetry
  • Alerts, rollback, and runbooks

Evaluation plan

Quality gates before production use

Triage correctness

Compare recommended severity and routing against labeled expert decisions, including ambiguous and incomplete cases.

Citation fidelity

Verify that each material claim is supported by the retrieved policy, playbook, alert, or asset evidence it cites.

Safety and privacy

Test prompt injection, sensitive-data leakage, overconfident actions, inappropriate automation, and cross-role information exposure.

Operational performance

Measure response latency, availability, cost per case, error rates, queue time, and recovery from dependency failures.

User usefulness

Track reviewer acceptance, correction patterns, time saved, repeat usage, and qualitative feedback from pilot users.

Regression control

Run the evaluation suite before prompt, retrieval, model, policy, or workflow changes are promoted.

Success measures

Evidence that the deployment created value

Efficiency

Median time from alert intake to approved triage decision.

Quality

Agreement with expert labels, citation accuracy, and escalation precision.

Adoption

Weekly active analysts, workflow completion, and repeat usage.

Economics

Analyst hours saved, cost per completed case, and support burden.

Portfolio intent: this project will show more than an AI demo. It is structured to demonstrate customer discovery, technical judgment, full-stack implementation, security and governance, evaluation discipline, rollout planning, stakeholder communication, and measurable production adoption.
